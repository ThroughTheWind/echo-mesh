# Echo 0.2.0: groups, and a way out

The second release, for testing with a few people you know, in person. It adds affinity groups,
votes on reports, position sharing within a group, a panic wipe and discreet mode. It is **still not
ready for a real protest**: it has had no outside security review and no field test in a crowd.

## What it does

- Everything in 0.1.0: phones running Echo find each other over Bluetooth LE, with no internet,
  server or account, and pass signed, expiring reports to each other on an offline map.
- **Groups.** Create a group, show its join code, and let a friend standing next to you scan it.
  The code works once and for two minutes, and every join is shown to the whole group. Group
  reports are encrypted: every phone relays them, only members can read them, and they show as
  verified.
- **Votes.** Confirm or dismiss a report; the votes spread like reports do.
- **Position sharing**, inside a group only, opt-in and for a limited time.
- **Panic wipe** in two actions, and **discreet mode** with a Quick Settings tile.
- English, with **draft** French and Spanish (see below).

## Install

1. Download `echo-0.2.0.apk` from this page and check its SHA-256 against the one below.
2. Open it on your phone and allow installing from this source when Android asks. It installs over 0.1.0.
3. Android 12 or newer.

## What it does not protect

- Anyone nearby with a radio receiver can tell your phone is running Echo, and that it sends
  group traffic, though not which group or what it says.
- Anyone who joins your group reads everything the group sends from then on, sees the positions
  members share, and can pass all of it on. Only show the join code to people you trust, in person.
- Nobody can be removed from a group. If a member's phone is taken, or a member turns out not to
  be who you thought, make a new group in person.
- Known group bugs: a phone that scans a second code for a group it is already in shows up twice
  on the others' member list, and a phone that leaves stays on the list. The list can look bigger
  than the group is.
- Public reports can be fake, and their confirmations and dismissals can be faked in bulk. Check
  before you act on one.
- After a panic wipe, Android still lists the permissions Echo was given, so the phone shows that
  Echo was set up.
- The French and Spanish texts are drafts that no fluent speaker has reviewed, including the safety
  explanations. Where it matters, read them in English.
- No independent review has checked the code yet (SECURITY.md), and the app has not been tested in
  a real crowd. Do not rely on it where being wrong could hurt you.
