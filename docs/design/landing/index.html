How it works

Echo isn't a chat app. It's a map that every phone nearby helps keep up to date, even when the phone network is down or overloaded.

  1. 1

    Get the map before you go

    Download your area at home, or import a map file a friend shares. On the street, Echo needs no connection at all.

  2. 2

    Turn on the mesh

    Your phone listens for other phones running Echo and passes reports along, even with the screen off.

  3. 3

    Glance, then act

    See what's around you at arm's length. Drop a report where it happened, and it spreads from phone to phone.

What it does

  • Drop a report on the map in a few taps: police line, medic, water, exit open.Available
  • Reports hop from phone to phone nearby, with no server in between.Available
  • Every report has a short lifetime, then every phone drops it.Available
  • Works on an offline map of your area that you get before you go.Available
  • Confirm a report is still true, or say it's gone, and see how many nearby phones agree.Available
  • Share your position with your group only, and only while you turn it on.Available
  • Discreet mode: darkest screen, no sound, no vibration, neutral notifications.Available
  • In English, French and Spanish.Available

Read at arm's length

Shape and colour both say what a report is, so it reads in sunlight, at night, and without telling colours apart. The ring shows how long it has left.

  • Danger

    A diamond. Police line, tear gas, kettling.

  • Blocked

    A square. Barrier, street closed.

  • Help and exits

    A circle. Medic, water, exit open.

  • Time left

    The ring empties as a report gets old. When it's gone, the report is gone from every phone.

What Echo protects, and what it doesn't

Read both lists before you rely on Echo. They use the same words as the app, and each line comes from the threat model.

It protects

  • Group reports, positions and nicknames can be read only by people who joined the group in person.Available
  • A group report shows which member sent it, and nobody can fake another member's report.Available
  • Nothing you send carries your name, number or a fixed ID, and Echo's radio signals change regularly.Available
  • Echo keeps no history of where you or others were. Reports disappear when they expire.Available
  • A panic wipe makes everything Echo stored unreadable within 2 seconds.Available
  • There's no server, no account, and no internet use except a map download you start yourself.Checked by the build

It doesn't protect

  • Anyone nearby with a radio receiver can tell your phone is running Echo.Known limit
  • Public reports can be fake, and their confirmations can be faked in bulk.Known limit
  • Anyone in your group can read everything sent to it, see shared positions, and pass them on. If a member's phone is taken, assume the group is exposed and make a new one in person.Known limit
  • If your phone is taken while unlocked, whoever has it can read your groups and post as you until you wipe it or your group makes a new one.Known limit
  • Someone with many receivers may guess roughly where a report was first sent.Known limit
  • Downloading a map reveals the region to your network and the map host.Known limit

Don't take our word for it

Echo was reviewed by people outside the project before this release. Everything it is and isn't is written down in the open, and the build refuses code that breaks its rules.

  • An independent security review of the protocol, the cryptography and the code.Available
  • Who signs Echo, and how to check that a download is really theirs, is published.Available
  • No ads, no analytics, no crash reports and no Google Play Services.Checked by the build
  • Free and open source under GPL-3.0: anyone can read the code and build it.By design

Read the threat model, the no-servers decision and the roadmap. Found a weakness? Report it privately.

What Echo won't do

Every feature is something that can leak or break. These stay out on purpose.

  • Chat. Briar already does messaging without servers; Echo carries reports.Not planned
  • Photos, video or voice. They carry faces and metadata that put people at risk.Not planned
  • Relays over the internet or satellite. A bridge is a place to watch from.Not planned
  • Suggested routes out. The mesh can't know a street is clear, and a wrong route hurts people.Not planned
  • iPhone. Apple limits the background radio a mesh depends on.Not planned

Questions

How do I get ready for a protest?

At home, the day before: install Echo, get the map of the area, and read both lists above. Join your group in person and try a report together. On the day, turn on the mesh when you arrive.

Can the police tell I'm using it?

Yes, if they have a radio receiver nearby. It's the first line of what Echo doesn't protect. If being seen with it is a risk where you are, keep the mesh off.

Does Echo hide who I am?

Nothing you send carries your name, number or a fixed ID, and there is no account to make. That isn't the same as being impossible to find: someone with many receivers may guess roughly where a report was first sent. Both are in the lists above.

Can a report be fake?

Yes. Anyone nearby can send a public report, and confirmations can be faked in bulk. Treat a public report as a tip, not a fact, and look at its age.

Why not use Signal or Briar?

Use them for messages. Signal needs the internet, and Briar is built for conversations. Echo does one different thing: a shared map of short-lived reports that every phone nearby can see.

How do I know I have the real app?

Get Echo only from this project's GitHub releases. Each release says who signed it and how to check that your download is theirs.

Does this page track me?

No. It runs no script, sets no cookies and loads nothing from other sites. Like every website, the server that hosts it sees your IP address. Links from here don't tell the next site where you came from.
